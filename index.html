HTML5 HEADER SECTION

The Article Area

This element represents an independent piece of content of a document, such as a blog entry, magazine or newspaper article, user comments, interactive widget, or any item of content.

Section Area I

Sections are used to group related content together thematically. While there's debate about when to use section versus div, the general consensus is that sections should be used when the content represents a distinct thematic grouping that would logically appear in a document outline. Sections typically have a heading and represent a meaningful subdivision of your content.

Section Area II

When deciding between multiple sections within an article, consider whether each section represents a distinct theme or topic within the larger context. Each section can have its own heading structure, starting with an h3 if the article uses h2 for its main heading. This creates a clear hierarchy that helps both users and assistive technologies understand the structure and organization of your content.

The Aside Section

The aside can represent secondary content outside of an article, that is considered secondary to the main content in the article. The aside can be cool typography designs, quotes, advertising, etc... The aside can also be utilized within an article, although in this instance there should be some parallel to the content of the article.

Although it may look like a sidebar in the sense of a navigational widget, it is always wise to use the nav element instead of an aside when you would like to place additional navigational elements.
